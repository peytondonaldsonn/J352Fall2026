8:46 a.m.

Flight 11 strikes the North Tower

American Airlines Flight 11 crashes into the North Tower of the World Trade Center.

Flight 11 was traveling from Boston to Los Angeles. The plane struck floors 93–99 of the North Tower, beginning the attacks in Lower Manhattan.

Flight 175 hits the South Tower

A second hijacked plane crashes into the World Trade Center, making clear that the first crash was part of a coordinated attack.

United Airlines Flight 175, also traveling from Boston to Los Angeles, strikes floors 77–85 of the South Tower. Television cameras capture the impact live.

9:05 a.m.

The President is informed

President George W. Bush is told that a second plane has struck the World Trade Center.

Bush was visiting an elementary school in Sarasota, Florida. Chief of Staff Andrew Card informed him about the second crash.

Flight 77 strikes the Pentagon

A third hijacked aircraft crashes into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia.

9:59 a.m.

The South Tower collapses

The South Tower collapses after burning for less than an hour following the plane's impact.

The South Tower collapses approximately 56 minutes after Flight 175 struck it. The collapse transforms the emergency response in Lower Manhattan.

Flight 93 crashes in Pennsylvania

Passengers and crew attempt to regain control of United Airlines Flight 93 before it crashes near Shanksville.

Flight 93 was headed toward Washington, D.C. The 9/11 Commission concluded that passengers and crew fought back against the hijackers, preventing the aircraft from reaching its intended target.

The North Tower collapses

The North Tower collapses, marking the destruction of both World Trade Center towers.

A massive rescue effort begins

Firefighters, police officers, emergency workers and civilians search for survivors amid the destruction in Lower Manhattan.
